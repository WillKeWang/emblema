II · Temperance of the Sundial

Adjuster · narrow function · mixed-initiated
Tarot · Temperance Instrument · Sundial Scope: narrow Initiative: mixed
"Mensura ante actum" — "Measure before action."

What kind of AI agent

You type a query and press enter, but instead of an answer the tool asks one question back: did you mean the original dataset, or the revised one? You had not said. You answer, and now the result is right.

That is Temperance. You use AI for narrow, well-defined moves, much like the Magician, but you want the system to interrupt inside the move: to ask a clarifying question, flag an ambiguity, or offer an alternative before it commits. You will accept a little friction in exchange for the system catching a mistake you were about to make. The whole art is calibration: enough friction to catch the costly errors, not so much that it nags on the routine ones.

The Tarot archetype

Temperance is the angel pouring liquid between two vessels — the act of moderating exchange. The card represents calibration, the patient art of the right proportion. The angel does not act alone and does not let the user act alone; she holds the moment in suspension.

The scientific instrument

The sundial — a time-measurement instrument calibrated to the local meridian. The gnomon's shadow encodes solar position; the hour-marks anchor the reading. The sundial is the clock as reference: it does not advance; it discloses where you already are. Calibration is its whole nature.

Where to start

  • Ask your everyday assistant to ask first. A standing instruction ("if my request is ambiguous, ask one clarifying question before answering") is a small change with a large effect on narrow tasks.
  • Favour tools that propose rather than impose. Spell-check, search reformulations, "did you mean" suggestions. Treat them as a counterpart you can accept or wave off in a second.
  • Watch the calibration. If a check keeps firing on things you meant on purpose (technical terms, unconventional usage, non-English names), turn it down for that context. Mis-aimed friction is worse than none.

Strength

Catches user mistakes inside the operation, without leaving the operation. The tightest reasonable feedback loop.

Signature failure mode

Pestering. The Adjuster's check overrides correct user intent when its heuristic is wrong — especially in domains the model misreads (technical vocabulary, non-English names, intentional unconventional usage). Mis-calibrated friction is worse than none.

What's at stake

Temperance treats the tool as something that can genuinely answer back, not a vending machine but a counterpart in a short exchange where each side holds half the decision. It works because you rarely spell out everything you mean: you know more than you say, and a well-timed question pulls the unspoken part into the open. The risk is a question asked at the wrong moment, about the wrong thing: friction that overrides a choice you actually intended.

Background reading: Buber on the I–Thou encounter, meeting the other as a counterpart capable of response rather than a thing to be used; Polanyi on tacit knowledge, that "we know more than we can tell" (The Tacit Dimension, 1966).

How you got here

The survey routes you to this card based on the following signals:

  • Q3 dominated by narrow operations (coding, writing, figures), scope score in the 1.3–1.7 range.
  • Q11 mixes "Correctness" or "Clear explanation of uncertainty" with operationally-balanced choices.
  • Q4 frustrations frequently include "overconfident answers" or "misunderstanding dataset," but the user is willing to accept friction in exchange for the check.
  • Often the largest cluster in the pilot: researchers using AI for narrow daily work and asking for more checks, not more autonomy.

A snapshot, not a verdict. This card reflects how you work with AI tools right now, which is shaped as much by what you have had access to as by what you would choose. How much friction you want usually shifts with the stakes of the task and your trust in the tool, so read Temperance as a current setting, not a fixed type.
