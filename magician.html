"Unum bene" — "One thing, well."

What kind of AI agent

The Magician's table holds many instruments, but he reaches for only one at a time. His power comes from discernment — knowing which tool fits this moment, and knowing when to put it back down.

Your use of AI is narrow, and the initiative stays almost entirely with you. You might let Copilot finish a line while you're writing code, or hand a sentence to ChatGPT to make it sound more natural. You don't hand off whole stretches of work. You just need a small action completed quickly.

Behind this habit there is often a kind of care for the thinking process itself. You're willing to use AI, but you keep it in a clear position — because you know which steps are worth doing yourself and which are just mechanical overhead. A lot of people spend time switching tools, checking details, handling small tasks. You care more about the parts that require actual judgment. For that reason, you naturally prefer help you can verify in a few seconds and retract just as fast. As long as you can confirm the result fits in moments, you can keep moving along your own line of thought without bending your rhythm to suit the tool.

This habit also tends to mean you've developed a stable sense of how you work. You rarely expect an outside system to define the problem for you — the problem usually already has a shape in your head. AI is most valuable to you when it appears when you need it and disappears when you don't. It completes a local action, the way a compass traces one arc, and keeps your attention on what actually matters. What you're often after is not more answers, but less friction — fewer interruptions to a line of thought you're already carrying.

The Tarot archetype

The Magician channels intent through chosen tools. He stands between heaven and earth wielding the four suit-objects — the card of deliberate craft: a single intent, a single instrument, a clean act. Mastery here is not in knowing many tools but in choosing the right one for the moment.

The scientific instrument

The compass — a drafting and mathematical instrument used by Newton, Galileo, and every architect since Vitruvius. It establishes geometric precision through a single fixed point and an arc. The compass turns intent into measurable form, and only one form per setting.

Strength

Trivially auditable. Predictable. The cleanest case for trust because the surface is small.

Signature failure mode

Brittleness. The Magician only works when you already know which tool to pick and how to wield it. Outside the operation's frame the instrument is silent — useless if you cannot name the spell, dangerous if you mis-name it.
