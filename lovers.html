V · The Lovers of the Astrolabe

Collaborator · broad function · mixed-initiated
Tarot · The Lovers Instrument · Astrolabe Scope: broad Initiative: mixed
"Coniunctim videmus" — "Together we see."

What kind of AI agent

You suggest an approach; the tool carries it a step further and flags a problem you had missed; you take half of that and redirect. An hour later the analysis is built, and you genuinely could not say which idea began with whom.

That is the Lovers. You work alongside AI at the scope of a whole phase, both of you contributing moves (proposing, refining, interrupting) until the phase is done. Broad scope, shared initiative: neither you nor the system carries the full weight of being right, and the work accumulates between turns. It is the way most research with AI actually happens today. What it asks of you is an audit trail: keeping track, across a long session, of which choices were yours.

The Tarot archetype

The Lovers card is canonically about aligned choice — two figures whose decisions stay in sync. The romantic reading is the surface; the deeper reading is partnership in pursuit of a shared aim. The card is the cleanest Tarot image of collaboration.

The scientific instrument

The astrolabe — astronomical-navigational instrument used from the 8th to the 17th century. The astrolabe encodes the celestial sphere as a stack of nested rotating brass plates; reading it is a joint act between the instrument and the reader's interpretation. Two hands easily turn the rete; one alone is awkward.

Where to start

  • Work where the history is visible. A tool with checkpoints, a chat log, or a diff per step lets a long back-and-forth leave a trail you can walk back through.
  • Close each session with a few lines. Note the decisions that mattered and who drove them. It costs a minute and saves the reproducibility headache later.
  • Do not carry forward what you cannot account for. When a turn produces something you do not fully understand, stop and close that gap before continuing. Accumulated unexamined work is the Lovers' real risk.

Strength

The most defensible position for daily research work — neither user nor system bears the full burden of correctness.

Signature failure mode

Ambiguity over who decided what. At the end of a long session you may not be able to reconstruct which choices were yours and which were the system's — a real reproducibility risk and a thin foundation for scientific authorship.

What's at stake

The Lovers is genuine partnership: the system is a real contributor to the work, not a tool you simply operate, and the result belongs to the pair. Treating the human and the system as equal members of one working network is part of what makes the collaboration productive. But science is judged from the outside, and an outside reviewer has to be able to see how a result was reached. The risk is muddled provenance: a finished phase that neither of you can fully break back into "your" decisions and "its" decisions, which is a thin foundation for authorship.

Background reading: Mollick's "centaur" pattern of human–AI collaboration (Co-Intelligence, 2024); Buber on the I–Thou partnership; Latour on the symmetry between human and non-human actors in a shared network (Reassembling the Social, 2005), with the proviso that the joint work stays legible to outside reviewers.

A snapshot, not a verdict. This card reflects how you work with AI tools right now, which is shaped as much by what you have had access to as by what you would choose. A great deal of everyday research with AI already sits at the Lovers, but how the work is split between you and the system shifts from task to task. Read it as a snapshot.
