"Coniunctim videmus" — "Together we see."

What kind of AI agent

The Lovers card is about alignment. Two people in the same scene, direction confirmed repeatedly, choices made in response to each other.

Your way of using AI hasn't settled into a strong fixed habit. You might work back and forth with Claude through an analysis phase — it proposes an angle, you think it's off, you redirect, it continues. You might build a module in Cursor, stepping in while it generates code, adjusting the logic until the result fits. You don't hand the whole phase off, and you don't hold every step tightly in your own hands.

Behind this is often a kind of practical flexibility. How much you trust AI shifts depending on the task — sometimes you're willing to let it run a few more steps, sometimes you want to intervene more. Working back and forth with AI is a natural state for you, not something you designed deliberately. You don't need it to stay completely quiet, and you don't need it to lead the whole process. You just want it to keep up with your thinking and give you something useful when you need it.

Over time, you find that many conclusions can't be attributed simply to either side, because they formed through repeated exchange. That experience tends to be engaging, and it often produces a feeling of being understood. The ability to keep getting value from that kind of back-and-forth also usually reflects an ability to organize scattered inputs into judgment. At the same time, you occasionally need to stop and look back at the original question — to confirm you're still holding the standard. The reason you get value from this kind of collaboration is precisely because you've always kept the ability to tell which direction is right.

The Tarot archetype

The Lovers card is canonically about aligned choice — two figures whose decisions stay in sync. The romantic reading is the surface; the deeper reading is partnership in pursuit of a shared aim. The card is the cleanest Tarot image of collaboration.

The scientific instrument

The astrolabe — astronomical-navigational instrument used from the 8th to the 17th century. The astrolabe encodes the celestial sphere as a stack of nested rotating brass plates; reading it is a joint act between the instrument and the reader's interpretation. Two hands easily turn the rete; one alone is awkward.

Strength

The most defensible position for daily research work — neither user nor system bears the full burden of correctness.

Signature failure mode

Ambiguity over who decided what. At the end of a long session you may not be able to reconstruct which choices were yours and which were the system's — a real reproducibility risk and a thin foundation for scientific authorship.
