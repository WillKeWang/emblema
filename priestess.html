What kind of AI agent

The High Priestess stands between two pillars, facing knowledge that hasn't fully revealed itself yet. She doesn't rush toward the answer — she lets the hidden lines surface slowly.

You want AI to work alongside you through the whole research project. You might want Claude to hold your project context over time, giving ongoing suggestions as you read papers, design experiments, and interpret results. You might want a research agent to proactively identify gaps in your experimental design and check in with you at key decision points.

Behind this is often a familiarity with problems that don't have standard answers. A lot of people use AI to reach conclusions faster. What you care about more is how the thinking unfolds — whether the question is genuinely seen. You want AI to hold your project context over time: the scattered conversations, the unverified hunches, the directions you've revised more than once. When an idea is still vague, you want it to catch what's worth pursuing further — to slowly bring out intuitions that are present but hard to articulate. What good collaboration feels like to you is continuous understanding, not one-time help.

What you actually weigh repeatedly is the question of boundaries. You're willing to give AI more room because you know complex work holds a lot of detail worth catching proactively. At the same time, you know that the most consequential judgments in a research project are often hidden inside choices that look small. You want it to raise angles you hadn't considered, flag risks you overlooked, even move the project forward — while you keep the final call. Because what you care about has never been only efficiency. You care more about whether the direction holds up under scrutiny. That's why you're particularly sensitive to whether AI actually understands what you're doing. Only when it has genuinely entered your line of thinking does the advice it gives have value.

The Tarot archetype

The High Priestess sits between two pillars, holding the scroll of the unwritten Torah. She is the card of hidden knowledge approached jointly — the gateway between the seen and the unseen. The Priestess does not reveal alone; she sits with the seeker.

The scientific instrument

The compound microscope made the unseen seeable, founding modern life sciences (van Leeuwenhoek, 1670s). The microscope is the canonical joint inquiry instrument: two parties — the slide and the eyepiece — must align for the object to be revealed.

Strength

The dream — joint goal-pursuit at full project scope, human-AI alignment maintained over time.

Signature failure mode

The audit problem. Hard to know who decided what; reproducibility nightmare; publication ethics genuinely unclear. The pilot's Q11 reproducibility-and-provenance cluster reads as a defensive response to this cell's risk.
