VII · The Fool of the Sextant

Deputy · whole task · user-initiated
Tarot · The Fool Instrument · Sextant Scope: whole Initiative: user
"Dimissus cum instrumento" — "Sent forth with the instrument."

What kind of AI agent

The Fool stands at the start of the journey with a bundle on his back. He's willing to walk into the unknown, but the direction stays in his hands.

Your research project is a road like that. AI accompanies you from start to finish, but on your rhythm. You might ask Claude to help design an ablation study — listing all the conditions to compare — but you go through each one yourself, check the scientific reasoning, and decide which are worth running and which don't add much. You have AI draft the full first version of your methods section, then revise from the first sentence, making sure every phrase accurately reflects your actual design choices. AI is there throughout, but the project's direction and every key judgment are yours.

Behind this is often a deep need for control. You're willing to use AI extensively and you know it saves time, but you find it hard to trust a system running continuously on its own. What you care about is usually not whether a given step ran fast enough, but whether the whole process lines up with your own judgment. You tend to understand the problem first, then decide direction — so even after AI gives you an answer, you instinctively ask why it did that, what it might have missed, what other paths exist. The real value, for you, is often in those moments of judgment, because you know that where a project ends up often depends on choices that looked minor in the middle.

People who work this way tend to have one thing in common: they rarely hand decision-making over easily. You may have noticed that the decomposing, comparing, and weighing that others find tedious is actually where you feel most settled. AI has taken away a lot of repetitive work, but it hasn't reduced your attention to the overall direction. So you often live in a particular state — efficiency has genuinely improved, but the sense of responsibility hasn't lightened. You stay at the center of the project, continuously calibrating the goal, checking for drift, correcting course. Over time that takes real energy, but it also means the final result is closer to what you actually wanted.

The Tarot archetype

The Fool sets out alone with a single bundle, looking at the horizon, not the void. The card is about the journey itself — taking on the whole road and accepting that no one else will walk it for you. It is not foolishness; it is the willingness to be the one acting at every step.

The scientific instrument

The sextant — a celestial-navigation instrument (Hadley, 1731) for measuring the angle between a star and the horizon. With a chronometer and a star almanac it gives the navigator latitude and longitude. The sextant is the chosen instrument the navigator carries: the journey is theirs to make, the sextant is leverage on each fix along the way. Harrison's longitude solution required one fix at a time, each one read and decided by a person.

Strength

Maximum user agency at whole-project scope. You stay the decision-maker throughout, and nothing happens that you did not choose.

Signature failure mode

Maximum cognitive load on the user. Because no system delegates, every structural decision — what to do next, what to verify, what to prioritize — has to come from you. The Fool's failure is not drift but exhaustion.
